At one worker admission costs 90 ms with almost no throttling and about 17–32 ms of webhook CPU, so
most of that baseline is waiting, probably on the webhook's two live LISTs. What grows with workers

run does not. Four workers is now the default, so this is the shipped configuration.

### The webhook falls over at 20 writes per second

The first seeded attempt, in run 45641195655, created seeds at 20 per second. With about 800 created,
the `frr-k8s-statuscleaner` container was OOM-killed against its 128Mi limit. It was killed 15 times
and crash-looped for the next hour, and kube-burner failed object verification. Each admission lists
every existing FRRConfiguration, so its memory grows with the objects and with the admissions in
flight. At 5 per second and 1,000 seeds its sampled peak was 26 MiB. The samples are 30 s apart, so
they miss short spikes. Both limits come from the default frr-k8s manifest.

## Scaling model

A reconcile's cost is its apiserver writes, one per node for an RA that needs them, so total work divided by writes should be constant. Total work is reconciles x reconcile mean, which also counts passes that write nothing. When every item is queued at once and drained by one worker, an item waits on average for half the work ahead of it, so wait divided by total work should sit near 0.5. Fitted on the single-worker, unseeded lanes that are not churn lanes: churn queues its items over time rather than in one burst, and a seeded lane writes against a different number of existing objects.

## Server-side cost of a write

Each RouteAdvertisements reconcile writes one FRRConfiguration per node, and the controller spends almost none of that time on CPU. Admission is the frr-k8s webhook's share of a write; the write mean is the whole request as the apiserver sees it. Both are count-weighted means over the RA job. CPU per write integrates each component's CPU over the job and divides by the FRRConfigurations written, so it includes the job's idle time and overstates small lanes slightly. Throttled is the share of the webhook container's CFS periods in which it hit its CPU limit.
